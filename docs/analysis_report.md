# Results Report: Checkout Redesign A/B Test and Channel Attribution

*All numbers come from `results/ab_metrics.json` and `results/attribution_metrics.json`. Data is synthetic and seeded.*

## Part 1. A/B test

### Recommendation

**Ship the one-page checkout, prioritising mobile, and monitor refunds for the first month.** Conversion and revenue per visitor both rose, and the effect is statistically distinguishable from zero. The size of the lift is uncertain, the refund guardrail passed only narrowly, and the evidence for a mobile-only effect is strong but not conclusive.

### Headline results

| Metric | Control | Treatment | Difference (95% CI) | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conversion rate | 5.14% | 5.47% | +0.33 pts (+0.08 to +0.58); **+6.4% relative** (+1.5% to +11.5%) | 0.0098 |
| Revenue per visitor | $4.85 | $5.43 | +$0.58 (bootstrap $0.28 to $0.88); +11.9% relative | 0.0002 (Welch) |
| Average order value | $94.38 | $99.23 | +$4.85 | 0.005 |
| Refund rate (guardrail) | 5.13% | 5.50% | +0.38 pts (-0.72 to +1.46); margin +1.5 | 0.50; non-inferiority passed narrowly |

Sample: 61,857 control and 62,143 treatment users, versus 48,348 required per arm.

### Trust checks

- Sample ratio: 61,857 vs 62,143, p = 0.42. No mismatch.
- Covariate balance: all p-values above 0.15.
- Novelty: relative lift was +8.5% in week 1 and +4.3% in week 2; the difference is not statistically significant (p = 0.41), so there is no evidence of decay, but two weeks is short.

### Who benefits

| Segment | Control | Treatment | Lift (pts, 95% CI) | Relative | Holm p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile | 4.42% | 4.96% | **+0.54 (+0.24 to +0.84)** | +12% | 0.0025 |
| Desktop | 6.36% | 6.38% | +0.02 (-0.43 to +0.48) | +0.4% | 1.0 |
| Tablet | 5.29% | 5.07% | -0.22 (-1.33 to +0.89) | -4% | 1.0 |
| New customers | 3.97% | 4.23% | +0.25 (-0.02 to +0.53) | +6% | 0.26 |
| Returning customers | 7.29% | 7.76% | +0.47 (-0.03 to +0.96) | +6% | 0.26 |

Only mobile survives multiple-testing correction. The `variant x device` interaction test is borderline (p = 0.052), so "mobile only" should be confirmed rather than assumed.

### Business impact

At an assumed 3.0M visitors per year, the CUPED-adjusted RPV difference implies about **$1.7M** of incremental annual revenue (95% CI roughly $0.9M to $2.6M).

### Methodological notes

- **CUPED** used pre-period spend as a covariate. The correlation with post-period revenue is only 0.17, so it cut variance by about 3%: cheap, but not a large lever here.
- **Bootstrap and Welch** intervals agree for revenue, which is skewed and zero-inflated.
- **Winner's curse:** the largest observed effects (mobile, RPV) are likely somewhat overstated. Plan the post-launch check with that in mind.

### Next steps

1. Launch to 100% with refund-rate monitoring (4-week checkpoint).
2. Run a mobile-only follow-up or holdout to confirm the heterogeneity.
3. Re-estimate revenue impact from the post-launch holdout, not from this test.

## Part 2. Marketing attribution

### Recommendation

**Do not cut display or paid social on last-touch evidence.** Last-touch shows both below break-even; a data-driven model shows them above it because they start journeys that other channels close.

| Channel | Last-touch ROAS | Linear ROAS | Markov ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | 15.5 | 10.4 | 11.9 |
| Organic search | 3.1 | 4.3 | 4.1 |
| Affiliate | 2.1 | 2.8 | 3.7 |
| Paid search | 2.9 | 3.2 | 3.0 |
| Display | **0.3** | 1.5 | **1.8** |
| Paid social | **0.7** | 1.6 | **1.7** |

(ROAS = attributed revenue per $1 of spend; spend figures are illustrative assumptions.)

### Findings

- 75% of conversions involve more than one channel, so the attribution model matters.
- Under last-touch, direct receives 38% of credit and display 1%. Under the Markov model direct falls to 23% and display rises to 7%, about **6x** its last-touch credit. Paid social gets 2.4x its last-touch credit.
- Display has an assist-to-close ratio of 14.5: it appears in earlier touches far more often than it closes.
- The Markov model's channel credit matches the known truth closely (correlation 0.9996, largest error 0.3 pts). Last-touch misallocates about 20% of credit and first-touch about 19%; Markov misallocates under 1%. This validation is favourable by construction because the journeys are generated by a first-order Markov process.

### Caveats

- Attribution allocates observed conversions; it does not measure incrementality. Validate the largest disagreements (display, paid social) with a geo or holdout test, analysed as in Part 1.
- Real journeys have untracked touches and selection effects (people who already intend to buy click email and branded search).
